About me

I'm a chemical engineering student at Tecnológico de Monterrey who wants to build a career in pharmaceutical engineering. So far my research has explored two corners of that field: how drug molecules crystallise, and how light can tell chiral molecules apart.

From June to August 2026 I was a research intern at Imperial College London's Surfaces and Particle Engineering Laboratory. I studied how L-arginine, used as a soft template, and buffer concentration affect the formation and morphology of vancomycin crystals, and programmed an Opentrons OT-2 robot to run those screens systematically.

Since my first semester I've also worked on Sum Frequency Generation Circular Dichroism (SFG-CD), a nonlinear optical technique for telling enantiomers apart. I crystallised mandelic acid samples and assembled the setup at Colgate University, won first place at the 2026 AIChE Southwest Regional Student Paper Competition, and I'm now preparing to present the work again in November 2026 against the other regional winners.

For my PhD I'm looking at chemical engineering applied to pharmaceuticals, and I'm open to different areas within it. At Tecnológico de Monterrey, I also teach review sessions on Material and Energy Balances as Academic Affairs Director and serve as vice president of the Professional Development Program.

Pharmaceutical crystallisation

2025 – 2026

Jun – Aug 2026, Imperial College London

L-arginine as a soft template for vancomycin crystallisation

How a drug crystallises shapes its purity, stability and how easily it can be processed. In the Surfaces and Particle Engineering Laboratory, I tested whether L-arginine, added as a soft template, could steer how the antibiotic vancomycin crystallises, and how buffer concentration changed crystal formation, morphology and reproducibility.

  • Vapour diffusion
  • Soft templating
  • Opentrons OT-2

Jun – Aug 2025, Colgate University

Crystallising mandelic acid enantiomers

I grew crystals of each mandelic acid enantiomer by hot filtration so they could be measured with SFG-CD. It's where my two research lines meet: the crystals come from chemical engineering, the measurement from optics.

Many drugs are chiral too, and their two enantiomers can act very differently in the body.

Light–matter interactions

2024 – present

Oct 2024 – Present, Tecnológico de Monterrey and Colgate University

First steps toward the characterization of enantiomers with SFG-CD

Enantiomers share almost every physical property, which makes them hard to tell apart. This project combines Second Harmonic Generation and Sum Frequency Generation to see whether mandelic acid enantiomers respond differently to circularly polarized light. I'm continuing the research ahead of a second technical presentation in November 2026.

Jan 2026 – Present

Circularly polarized Laguerre–Gaussian beams in turbulent media

Does circular polarization help a twisted beam of light get through turbulence? I'm building MATLAB simulations to find out. The pattern at the top of this page comes from the same family of beams.

  • Model the propagation and transmission of circularly polarized Laguerre–Gaussian beams through turbulent optical media.
  • Compare transmitted optical power across turbulence conditions and polarization states to test whether circular polarization improves transmissivity.
